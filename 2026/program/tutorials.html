Tutorial 1

Time-Series Foundation Models for Dynamic World Modeling: Principles and Frontiers

Time-series data describe the dynamic evolution of the real world and are widely used in transportation, energy, environment, industry, finance, and urban computing. Traditional task-specific modeling limits knowledge transfer across datasets, domains, and tasks. Time-Series Foundation Models (TSFMs), enabled by large-scale pre-training, unified representations, and zero-/few-shot adaptation, have formed a new paradigm for general-purpose temporal intelligence, expanding from forecasting to anomaly detection, multimodal analysis, question answering, and reasoning. This tutorial introduces TSFMs from the perspective of Dynamic World Modeling. Organized around the AGREE principles (Automation, Generalization, Robustness, Explainability, and Efficiency), the tutorial discusses generative and multimodal modeling, spatio-temporal applications, and temporal reasoning. Building on advanced research in human mobility analytics, spatio-temporal intelligence, and cross-modal time-series analytics, it aims to help researchers establish a complete understanding from traditional forecasting toward general-purpose temporal intelligence and Dynamic World Modeling.

Tutorial 2

Towards an Efficient System for Geospatial Analytics: A Hands-on Tutorial

Geospatial analytics is an important field in many application domains, including epidemiology, transportation science, criminology, and urban science. Therefore, many software packages and systems, e.g., QGIS, ArcGIS, SuperMap, and spatstat, can also support these geospatial analysis tools. However, since (1) these tools suffer from high time complexities and (2) many large-scale location datasets can be collected by domain experts nowadays, these software packages and systems are inefficient (or even infeasible) to support large-scale location datasets. As such, we conduct this tutorial to arouse the interest from those spatiotemporal data management researchers and practitioners for (i) developing efficient algorithms for these tools and (ii) using our fast QGIS plugins and our preliminary system, based on our efficient algorithms, to analyze their large-scale location datasets, respectively. This tutorial consists of four parts. In the first part, we will provide an overview of geospatial analysis tools. In the second part, we will specifically discuss efficient algorithms for kernel density visualization (one of the tools) and its variants, which can possibly be extended to support other tools. In the third part, we will provide hands-on experience for testing the efficiency of existing software packages/systems and our QGIS plugins/preliminary system (by conducting some case studies). In the fourth part, we will discuss the future trends of this topic.

Tutorial 3

LLM-Powered Question Answering: Planning and Optimization Through a Query Processing Lens

Large language models (LLMs) are expanding the capabilities of question answering over textual and tabular data. As questions become more complex, effective QA systems require careful planning and a balance between computational cost and answer quality. This tutorial examines LLM-powered question answering through the lens of query processing. We introduce logical and physical planning as a unifying framework for understanding how questions are transformed into executable workflows. We review representative approaches and discuss how alternative execution strategies shape cost–quality trade-offs. Particular emphasis is placed on the opportunities and challenges of applying query optimization principles to LLM-powered QA. The tutorial provides researchers and practitioners with a structured perspective on system design and highlights open directions in planning and optimization.

Tutorial 4

Blockchain-Enhanced Federated Learning for Trustworthy Edge Intelligence

Edge computing enables intelligent services to be deployed close to data sources, but collaborative learning across distributed edge devices and servers remains challenging due to data heterogeneity, privacy constraints, unreliable participants, and cyber threats. Federated learning provides a natural mechanism for collaborative model training without centralising raw data, yet conventional flat FL architectures are often insufficient for realistic multi-layer edge environments. This tutorial presents a unified view of blockchain-enhanced federated learning for trustworthy edge intelligence, centred on a two-layer hierarchical federated architecture that mirrors the physical organisation of edge computing systems. We progressively examine how relationships among distributed participants can be exploited to improve edge-level aggregation, and how validation, reputation, auditing, and blockchain-supported coordination can strengthen global collaboration against unreliable or malicious participants. These concepts are integrated through representative research on graph-informed federated learning and blockchain-secured hierarchical FL. The tutorial concludes with a hands-on session using the RBHFL testbed, where participants will explore hierarchical training, aggregation, validation, attack simulation, and blockchain-assisted coordination, as well as possible extensions toward future trustworthy federated edge systems.
